TF-IDF led on the main collected test

On these 1,000 held-out English emails, TF-IDF detected 491 positive-labeled messages and missed 9. DistilBERT detected 417 and missed 83. The LLM produced no alerts at its frozen cutoff.

Read “positive detection” carefully. The original positive class includes spam, fraud and phishing. These numbers do not establish 98.2% recall on independently verified phishing alone.

MCC ranges from −1 to 1; ECE is calibration error on a 0–1 scale. They are shown to three decimals. A model that never alerts has undefined precision and MCC. Zero observed false alarms does not establish zero real-world risk.

Why the LLM’s 0% false alarms is not a win: it detected 0 / 500 positives and only 47.3% of its requested outputs were valid. Invalid outputs count as safe; valid calibrated scores also fell below the alert cutoff.

A separate, positive-only 100-email collected challenge recorded detections of 92% for TF-IDF, 17% for DistilBERT and 0% for the LLM. It has no negative class and shares a historical source family, so it cannot fill the independent two-class OOD gates.

The later check revealed a large false-alarm problem

The 400 submitted messages became 303 distinct inputs after deduplication. TF-IDF found all 173 positive scenarios, but it also flagged 125 of the 130 negative scenarios.

This is constructed evidence. Message bodies were generated, translated or substantially reconstructed. It is useful for functional stress testing, and cannot replace authentic independent OOD or adversarial email evaluation.

DistilBERT avoided false alarms here but missed 100 of 173 positives. The LLM missed all positives and returned valid output for only 116 / 303 inputs (38.3%). No threshold was retuned on this test.

What these charts can support

Supported conclusion

TF-IDF is the project’s chosen model. It gave the strongest detection/speed combination among the three models in the main English ID run. The constructed comparison exposes a substantial false-alarm risk.

Still unassessed

Authentic independent two-class OOD, validated adversarial email controls, Mac model performance and the complete installed-extension workflow. There is no qualified original-gate winner or survivor scorecard.

How to read the uncertainty

Whiskers and table parentheses are the originally recorded 95% group-bootstrap intervals. The report has not recomputed or tightened them. Empirical intervals can collapse to zero when no errors are observed; that does not prove the true error rate is zero. The 2% FPR alert target was chosen on validation only; the original test-ID FPR gate remains 3%.
