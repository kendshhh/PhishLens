The TF-IDF benchmark, explained simply

PhishLens · 29 October 2026

190 of 200 phishing emails caught 1 false alert among 1,000 legitimate emails*

*“Legitimate” means the source label. We have evidence for a controlled trial in the app; broader testing remains incomplete.

What to say aloud

We wanted to find a model that catches phishing without warning people about too many ordinary emails. We tested a new TF-IDF candidate on 1,200 emails that this project had not used for model development. It caught 190 of the 200 phishing-labelled emails and falsely flagged one of the 1,000 labelled legitimate emails. Those are useful results, with a clear limit: this test does not cover every real inbox, bank message or attack.

Why this matters: Lead with the outcome and its limits, so the audience knows what the evidence actually supports.

2 of 12

What the scanner does

1. Read the supplied text

The benchmark uses cleaned email text. The product will receive highlighted visible text.

2. Calculate a model score

TF-IDF turns text patterns into numbers. Logistic Regression combines the learned weights.

3. Apply the frozen alert rule

The system either raises an advisory alert or stays below its alert threshold.

4. Explain the result

Local code adds exact quotes, context, next steps and the things it could not verify.

A text scan cannot authenticate a sender or inspect unseen attachments.

What to say aloud

Think of this as a second opinion on the words supplied to it. The trained model calculates the alert. Separate local software writes explanations from patterns it can actually point to in the text. An email below the alert threshold is not proven genuine. The benchmark used prepared email text; the final extension still needs its own highlighted-text capture test.

Why this matters: Separate the trained classifier from the explanation software and from future extension integration.

3 of 12

How this model learns

TF-IDF represents the text

Words and short word pairs become numeric features.

The selected candidate also uses small character fragments.

Logistic Regression learns weights

Training examples teach it which combinations lean toward the phishing label.

The exported model keeps those learned weights for local browser scoring.

This is trained machine learning. It does not need a chatbot or cloud service.

What to say aloud

TF-IDF describes the wording numerically. Logistic Regression learns how to combine those numbers using labelled training mail. We tried word features alone and word features with character fragments. Character fragments can represent parts of unusual spellings, but this experiment did not isolate their individual benefit. The final output also includes rule-based observations; those observations are separate from the learned classifier.

Why this matters: Explain the AI work without implying that a larger language model wrote the insights.

Six means six predetermined ways to train TF-IDF and Logistic Regression, not six unrelated AI families. Each configuration went through five grouped development rounds, producing 30 nested fits, and we then made six full-development exports. The first policy allowed too many false alerts. What to say aloud

The test has four possible outcomes. A phishing email that alerts is a correct detection. A phishing email without an alert is a miss. A labelled legitimate email with an alert is a false alarm. A labelled legitimate email without one is a correct non-alert. Accuracy is 99.08%, but the separate recall and false-alert rates tell us more. A model could look accurate by ignoring phishing when most messages are legitimate. Confidence intervals describe uncertainty within this research sample, not every future inbox.

Why this matters: Make every headline percentage traceable to a count and expose the remaining errors.

Questions a panel may ask

This guide covers the main methods, results, limitations and implementation questions. It cannot anticipate every possible question. When evidence is missing, say so.

1. What was the main question?

Purpose

Can a small local TF-IDF system catch phishing while reducing false warnings on ordinary mail? The follow-up focused on false-alarm reduction and a usable browser path. It did not restart the entire multi-model benchmark.

2. Which model did we choose?

Purpose

Word-plus-character TF-IDF with Logistic Regression, C=1, under the separately approved v12a conservative alert policy. It was selected on development evidence before the final test.

3. Does TF-IDF count as AI?

Purpose

TF-IDF is the representation, and Logistic Regression is the trained machine-learning classifier. Together they learn from labelled data. The insight text comes from local software rules and feature contributions, not an LLM. If a course explicitly requires LLM or RAG functionality, these benchmark results alone do not establish compliance with that requirement.

4. Why use a small model instead of a larger LLM?

Purpose

This project needs local browser scoring with small files and low latency. The selected candidate met those measured constraints. This particular test compared TF-IDF variants only, so it does not prove that TF-IDF beats every LLM or neural model.

5. Where did the final emails come from?

Data

The 1,000 ham labels came from the Kaggle compilation of Enron (594), SpamAssassin (121), Ling (135) and CEAS_08 (150). The 200 phishing labels came from the Nazario collector family, including its Kaggle mirror (36) and original 2020–2024 files (164). Treat the mirror and original as one collector family.

6. Who decided which emails were phishing?

Data

The source or publisher supplied the labels. The pipeline preserved them. An AI assistant did not create new binary ground truth. Publisher labels can still be wrong, and ham labels do not authenticate sender identity or certify safe behavior.

7. Did the final test use AI-generated emails?

Data

No synthetic mail was admitted into the primary final cohort. Historical synthetic fixtures did not replace these collected-source inputs. The 41 reused security notices in development have collector-attested provenance rather than independent sender authentication; they do not establish fresh bank coverage.

8. Were all spam emails treated as phishing?

Data

No. The primary positive task used phishing-specific Nazario publisher labels. Generic spam and broad fraud positives were excluded. This keeps the label definition closer to the project question.

9. How much data did each stage use?

Data

Training/development used 27,391 rows: 25,311 ham and 2,080 phishing. Calibration used 600: 500 ham and 100 phishing. Cutoff validation used 1,100: 1,000 ham and 100 phishing. Final confirmation used 1,200: 1,000 ham and 200 phishing. Another 364 records remained reserved.

10. Why was this not a 70/15/15 split?

Data

The separately approved follow-up allocated fixed, distinct roles with enough legitimate validation examples and a protected final cohort after historical-exposure checks. These are the documented v12/v12a allocations. Presenting them as the original 70/15/15 split would be inaccurate.

11. What does fresh test mean?

Data

Unused for this project’s model fitting, development choices or prior scoring before this confirmation. It does not mean recently collected. The data remain historical, and training includes 2025 mail while final positives include 2020–2024, so this is not a forward-time test.

12. How did you avoid testing on training copies?

Data

The pipeline checked historical use and grouped copies, message families and shared 30-word passages across full and shortened text. It corrected failed allocations before model scoring. Frozen role hashes and final disjointness checks confirm the admitted files did not change. This reduces detected leakage without proving all semantic overlap is impossible.

13. Why keep similar messages in one group?

Data

If one copy teaches the model and a near copy appears in the exam, the model may appear better than it is. Keeping the family together reduces that shortcut. The final 1,200 records form 1,135 groups.

14. How did you handle long messages?

Data

The shared cleaning step normalizes inert HTML/whitespace and privacy fields, then keeps the first 2,000 plus last 300 Unicode codepoints when needed. It cut 190 of the final 1,200 inputs. The model did not receive an extra model-specific token cut. The public text adapter rejects raw HTML.

15. Was every final input English?

Data

A statistical English filter admitted the cohort, but error inspection found a CSS-heavy input with German text. It stays in the results. The defensible description is English-filtered data with a known language-filter error, not a perfectly pure English set.

16. Is the test free of bias?

Data

No dataset can justify that absolute claim here. Positive examples share one collector family and ham comes from other collections, so collection style can correlate with the label. Legacy dates, source labels, artificial test prevalence and limited controls also restrict generalization. Grouping, declared policies and held-out testing reduce specific biases, not every bias.

17. What were the six configurations?

Method

Two text representations, words alone or words plus character fragments, each with LR C=0.5, 1 or 2. C controls regularization, which restrains fitted weights. The chosen word-plus-character export has 18,000 word and 12,000 character features.

18. Why are there 36 fits if there were only six options?

Method

Each option used five grouped development rounds: 6 × 5 = 30 nested classifier fits. Six full-development export fits followed. That is 36 fits, not 36 independent final tests and not 36 unrelated model families.

19. What did the first attempt get wrong?

Method

Every original v12 option failed development eligibility under a permissive cutoff policy. The lowest general development FPR was 4.322%, still above 3%. The best exposed-control result was 26 false alerts out of 41, from a different configuration. The saved failed selection remains unchanged.

20. Did you change the rules just to obtain a pass?

Method

After the original failure, the user approved a separately documented v12a follow-up before final scoring. It changed the operating and candidate-selection policy, reused the same model weights, and lowered none of the numeric gates. The original failure remains visible. This is an approved follow-up study, not a claim that the first study passed.

21. What exactly changed in v12a?

Method

The raw alert cutoff became max(0, the lowest validation cutoff giving FPR at most 2%). This conventional floor can make alerts more conservative. Among eligible candidates, the selection favored fewer exposed-control false alarms, then recall and size/simplicity. v12a added no classifier-weight fits.

22. Does a 2% target require exactly 20 false alarms out of 1,000?

Method

No. It is a ceiling for validation, not a quota. Zero or one false alert is acceptable if the other requirements hold. The selected final rule gave zero false alerts among the 1,000 cutoff-validation ham messages before final testing.

23. Why choose C=1 when C=2 caught more development phishing?

Method

The approved selection prioritized false alarms on the exposed security controls. C=1 had 0/41 control alerts, while C=2 had 6/41. C=1 still met the development recall requirement. Those control results chose a candidate; they do not prove an independent bank/OTP pass.

24. What is score calibration?

Method

Calibration adjusts the mapping from the classifier’s raw margin to its displayed score using the separate 600-email pile. It does not retrain the text weights and does not turn the score into a guarantee about a real user’s inbox. ECE measures average score mismatch across bins in the research data.

25. Why is the alert boundary about 88.71% rather than 50%?

Method

The alert policy operates on raw margin, where the chosen boundary is zero. The separate calibration mapping turns that boundary into 88.7126%. A 50% displayed-score cutoff would be a different, untested operating policy. The UI must show the score and its decision context together.

26. When was the final system frozen?

Method

The final lock recorded hashes of the model, code, data, calibration, threshold and timing evidence before any of the 3,600 fresh predictions. Candidate selection also preceded that lock. Final verification found those frozen dependencies unchanged.

27. Were three different AI model families tested here?

Results

No. The final confirmation compared the frozen original TF-IDF reference, the same reference with validation recalibration, and the newly trained selected v12a TF-IDF candidate. Earlier DistilBERT/LLM results belong to other studies and cannot be fairly re-ranked using this cohort.

28. What were the final results in ordinary numbers?

Results

Out of 200 phishing labels, v12a caught 190 and missed 10. Out of 1,000 ham labels, it left 999 below threshold and falsely alerted one. The original caught 190 and falsely alerted 11. Recalibrating the old reference caught 189 and falsely alerted 11.

29. How do recall, FPR and accuracy differ?

Results

Recall counts phishing caught: 190/200 = 95%. FPR counts ham falsely alerted: 1/1,000 = 0.1%. Accuracy counts all correct decisions: (190 + 999)/1,200 = 99.08%. Report recall and FPR alongside accuracy because a class-heavy dataset can make accuracy look reassuring.

30. What is precision?

Results

Precision asks how many alerts match phishing labels. Here 190 of the 191 candidate alerts did: 99.48%. It depends on the phishing proportion. A real inbox with much less phishing can have a lower precision at the same recall and FPR.

31. Did the new model find exactly the same phishing as the old one?

Results

No. It recovered seven old misses and newly missed seven that the original caught. Both detected 190 overall. Equal recall does not mean identical behavior or identical failure modes.

32. Is v12a statistically proven better?

Results

The point results show ten fewer false alerts with the same recall total. The paired overall-accuracy test did not establish superiority after correcting three comparisons: original vs v12a adjusted p=0.1299. Lack of statistical significance does not prove equivalence. Its engineering selection followed development criteria chosen before final scoring.

33. What do the confidence intervals mean?

Results

The report resampled whole related-message groups 2,000 times, seed42. Candidate recall was 95%, with a 95% interval of 91.63%–97.93% within these sources. FPR was 0.1%, with a group-bootstrap interval of 0%–about 0.30%. A lower bound of zero does not prove the true future false-alarm rate is zero.

34. What do MCC, AUC and ECE add?

Results

MCC combines all four confusion cells: v12a scored 0.9668 on a −1 to 1 scale. ROC-AUC 0.99850 and PR-AUC/average precision 0.99502 describe ranking over many cutoffs. ECE 0.00542 describes binned calibration mismatch in this mixture. None replaces the chosen-cutoff recall/FPR checks. MCC does not mean Mac/macOS testing.

35. Did every original benchmark gate pass?

Results

No. The candidate passed eight measured scoped checks, including a separate complete-workflow speed check. All three TF-IDF systems passed those measured checks. Original independent OOD/adversarial roles remain unassessed, so the original full qualification and robustness-weighted ranking are incomplete.

36. If all three passed the measured checks, why prefer the new candidate?

Results

A gate sets a minimum. It does not rank every passing system. We selected v12a before final testing because the approved development policy prioritized fewer security-control false alerts. The final test then observed ten fewer false alerts with the same recall total. The original is faster in measured scoring; overall accuracy superiority was not established. There is no original full-benchmark weighted winner because independent robustness roles are missing.

37. What was the one remaining false alert?

Reliability

A CEAS_08 ham-labelled American Express/Fidelity 2007 annual bank statement. This is a concrete reason to keep fresh bank/OTP coverage open rather than claiming that banking false alarms are solved.

38. What phishing did the new model miss?

Reliability

Observed misses included attachment/document notices, banking lures with broken word boundaries, lures padded with unrelated content, a compensation scam, a CSS-heavy non-English preparation error, and a publisher-positive software-licensing thread with unresolved malicious intent. These are qualitative observations, not independently validated adversarial categories.

39. Did you remove embarrassing errors or relabel them after testing?

Reliability

No. The 1,200 inputs, publisher/source labels, model and cutoff remained unchanged. The language-filter error and label ambiguity stay in the metrics. Qualitative assistant inspection is not new ground truth or a human rating.

40. Were the speed numbers actual Chrome measurements?

Browser

Yes. Chrome156.0.8078.73 on Windows11 build26300, Intel Core i5-14450HX. Inference used JavaScript CPU. The candidate worker normal p95 was 1.169 ms. The worst measured candidate prepared-text workflow p95 was 6.673 ms on the directly throttled main-thread stress path.

41. What does p95 mean?

Browser

Ninety-five percent of the observed timing samples were at or below that number. Each cell timed 100 validation inputs after discarding 10 warmups. The study had 12 cells: three systems, two placements, normal/4× settings. This produced 1,200 timing observations, distinct from the 1,200-email accuracy cohort.

42. Does 4× mean every part of the laptop ran four times slower?

Browser

No. Chrome CDP slows the page thread and may not affect dedicated-worker computation. The benchmark added a direct-main-thread run of the same kernel under actual CDP slowdown. This provides measured stress evidence, not a full simulation of an older laptop.

43. Does the speed include the entire installed extension?

Browser

No. It includes prepared-text classification, software observations, attribution and quote checks, plus worker round-trip where used. It excludes capture, public input normalization, MIME preparation and DOM rendering. Installed-extension end-to-end performance still needs measurement.

44. What about cold start, memory, download and Mac testing?

Browser

Candidate worst cold readiness was 374.425 ms with local files and possibly warm OS caches. Required benchmark client assets were 2,579,649 bytes. The largest measured main-thread heap was 11,858,220 bytes; total worker/user-agent memory timed out. No Mac, WASM or WebGPU results were substituted. These are not Internet download timings.

45. Where do the reasons and tips come from?

Insights

Deterministic local software identifies supported text observations and adds why they matter and a practical next step. Exact linear contributions show up to three word features influencing the raw score. Character features also affect the decision, so the visible word list is partial attribution. No cloud LLM writes these explanations.

46. How do you know an explanation refers to the email?

Insights

Code checks each quoted span against the exact prepared analysis text and checks attribution arithmetic. Python/Chrome parity covered 52 development/validation inputs per system, and the integration package matched the candidate on those inputs. This verifies faithful quoting and computation, not that every judgement or tip is useful to a person.

47. Are the explanations proven clear and complete?

Insights

Not yet. Thirty error-enriched cards are prepared for actual human ratings of grounding, clarity, usefulness, completeness and unsafe advice. No ratings were invented. Error-enriched review is useful for finding problems but does not estimate the overall detection accuracy of an inbox.

48. What happens if the model fails?

Insights

The page shows Unable to assess, rather than a confident safe result. Blank and unsupported raw-HTML inputs were rejected, and an actual injected worker fault exercised the fallback. Benchmark accounting treats unusable outputs as non-alert/invalid and lowers validity; none occurred in the final 3,600 outputs.

49. Is a non-alert the same as a genuine email?

Delivery

No. It means only that the text did not cross the frozen alert rule. This system does not authenticate the sender, inspect unseen attachments or establish that a link is safe. A ham source label is also not proof of authenticity.

50. Has this already replaced the app model or gone live?

Delivery

No. The delivered package contains the evaluated weights and local browser path. The app provider, backend database and public deployment were not switched. Package parity is different from testing a fully installed Chrome extension with actual email capture and persistence.

51. What can we honestly say to professionals?

Delivery

We selected an advisory TF-IDF candidate before fresh testing to reduce false alarms. On the frozen 1,200-email source-labelled cohort it retained 95% recall and produced one false alert among 1,000 ham. It met measured Windows Chrome limits. Independent robustness, fresh banking controls, human explanation quality and extension integration remain open.

52. What remains before a broader release claim?

Delivery

Validate actual highlighted-text capture and quote mapping, installed-extension behavior/CSP/offline failures, and opt-in report saving. Obtain real human ratings of explanations. Separately evaluate fresh authenticated controls and independent modern/OOD or validated adversarial mail. Passing source-pair metrics cannot substitute for those claims.

53. Can we tune on these 1,200 messages now?

Delivery

They are now exposed test evidence. You can inspect their errors, but tuning on them would consume their role as fresh confirmation. Any later model or operating-policy change needs a newly frozen independent confirmation cohort. Keep the present results as history.

54. Where can someone verify the results?

Delivery

The benchmark folder contains summary.md, metrics.json, final_lock.json, run_record.json, Graph.csv, error_analysis.md and verification records. The visual report links the evidence. It reconciled 3,600 predictions, 5,393 CSV rows and 27,549 numeric CSV cells. Eleven unit tests passed. Those are different checks, not extra independent email tests.
