Benchmark terms in plain English

Read each number with its denominator and scope. No Mac measurements are available.

TF-IDF

A numeric representation of words or character fragments: more frequent in a message, less common across training messages, usually gives more weight.

Logistic Regression

A trained linear classifier. Its weights combine text features into a raw margin; a sigmoid can turn that margin into a model score.

Raw margin and cutoff

The weighted feature sum plus intercept. This candidate alerts at margin ≥0. Its calibrated displayed score at that boundary is about 88.7%, not 50%.

Calibration

A mapping fitted only on held-out calibration data to improve score reliability for that research distribution. It does not authenticate an email or guarantee an inbox probability.

Recall / false negative

Recall = TP/(TP+FN): phishing caught. Here 190/(190+10)=95%. A false negative is phishing-labelled input that did not alert.

FPR / false positive

FPR = FP/(FP+TN): ham falsely alerted. Here 1/(1+999)=0.1%. It is not the fraction of alerts that are wrong.

Precision

TP/(TP+FP): here 190/191=99.48%. This changes with the proportion of phishing in the population.

F1

Harmonic mean of precision and recall: 2TP/(2TP+FP+FN). It does not replace a separate false-alarm gate.

MCC

Matthews correlation coefficient combines all four confusion cells; −1 means inverse, 0 no association, 1 perfect prediction. It is not Mac/macOS evaluation.

ROC-AUC and PR-AUC

Ranking summaries across possible thresholds. Here PR-AUC is average precision. A high AUC does not alone mean the chosen alert cutoff passes.

ECE

Expected calibration error across 10 score bins: weighted difference between average predicted score and observed phishing fraction. Smaller is better on this dataset; binning and prevalence matter.

Group-bootstrap 95% CI

Resample whole related-message groups 2,000 times, seed42, retaining within-group dependence. This interval describes uncertainty within the admitted sources, not all future phishing.

McNemar / Holm

A paired comparison of which examples each system gets right. Holm corrects for three comparisons. Adjusted p=0.1299 did not establish an overall accuracy difference; it does not prove equality.

Development / calibration / threshold / final

Separate roles for choosing model settings, fitting score mapping, choosing the alert cutoff, and once-only confirmation. Final labels never fit the model or cutoff.

Fresh source-pair test

Unused project inputs drawn from named historical sources. It is not necessarily modern, forward-time, independently collected or representative of real inboxes.

OOD / adversarial

Out-of-distribution means a documented shift beyond development sources. Adversarial means validated evasive attack cases. Ordinary source-pair mail is not automatically either.

p50 / p95 / p99

Latency percentiles: respectively 50%, 95% and 99% of measured runs are at or below the value. These use 100 validation timings per cell after 10 discarded warmups.

Cold start

Navigation to the first valid local response in a fresh browser context. File/OS caches may remain warm. It does not measure an Internet model download or first extension install.

4× CPU throttle

Chrome DevTools page slowdown. Worker compute may be outside its effect; a separate direct-main-thread kernel stress run was therefore measured. Neither simulates a whole slower machine.

Evidence vs feature attribution

A quote can support an observable text pattern. A feature contribution explains the classifier arithmetic. Neither by itself proves malicious intent, sender identity or safe links.

Pass in scope vs full qualification

Eight measured checks passed for this source-pair Windows path. Missing independent OOD/adversarial, fresh bank controls and human quality cannot be recorded as passes.

Mac evaluation

No Mac/macOS run was performed in this study. Windows timings cannot be presented as Mac measurements.
